Rural employment · October 2026

The law now promises 125 days of work. Households were getting 48.

VB-G RAM G replaced MGNREGA on 1 July 2026. It raises the guarantee, but the old one was rarely met. The real limits were funding, state capacity and accountability, and the new law shifts more of the bill to states.

Recommendations

What would close the gap

Publish normative allocations and their formula.States should know their central ceiling before the year begins, so demand isn't turned away mid-year.
A transition fund for low-capacity states.Phase in the 40% share over 2–3 years for states whose own revenue is below the national median.
Make delay compensation automatic.Calculate it in the payment system and pay it without a claim. Manual claims have yielded only 2–8% of dues.
Fund Panchayat staffing for audits.Tie part of administrative funds to filling Panchayat Secretary posts, so every Gram Panchayat holds at least one social audit a year.
Clear MGNREGA dues on a published schedule.Report pending liabilities by state every quarter until they are cleared.
Revise the wage base year.Update the 2009-10 CPI-AL base so wages keep pace with inflation, as the Standing Committee recommended in 2024 and 2025.

Data protection · October 2026

A privacy law that leaves the government largely unchecked

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 gives people rights over their data and lets the regulator fine companies up to ₹250 crore per breach. But it lets the government exempt itself, keeps the regulator close to the executive, and weakens the Right to Information Act.

Recommendations

Safeguards to add before May 2027

Restore the public-interest test in RTI Section 8(1)(j).Personal information should stay disclosable where it relates to public activity, or where the public interest outweighs privacy.
Make the Board independent.Add the Chief Justice of India (or a nominee) and the Leader of the Opposition to its selection committee. Give members fixed tenure, and have the Board report to Parliament, not MeitY.
Limit Section 17 exemptions.Every exemption should be written, time-bound, necessary and proportionate, laid before Parliament and independently reviewed.
Add compensation.Let the Board award compensation to people harmed by a breach.
Publish an annual report.The Board should table yearly figures on complaints, penalties and government exemptions in Parliament.
