# From MGNREGA to VB-G RAM G: Implementation Gaps and Recommendations

Prashant Yadav · October 2026

## Summary

VB-G RAM G raises the rural work guarantee from 100 to 125 days. But under MGNREGA, households averaged only about 48 days a year, and fewer than 10% completed 100. So the binding constraint was never the legal ceiling. It was funding, state capacity and accountability. The new law shifts 40% of costs to most states and caps central funds at a normative allocation, which risks widening that gap in states with weak finances or capacity.

This brief recommends that Parliament track three things: whether states release their 40% share on time, whether unemployment allowance and delay compensation are actually paid, and whether social audits reach every Gram Panchayat.

## Background

Parliament passed the VB-G RAM G Act in December 2025 to replace MGNREGA (2005). It came into force nationwide on 1 July 2026. Key changes:

| Feature | MGNREGA | VB-G RAM G |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days guaranteed per household | 100 | 125 |
| Funding model | Demand-driven | Normative state-wise allocation set by the Centre; any excess borne by the state |
| Centre : state cost share | Centre paid 100% of wages, 75% of materials (about 90% of total spending) | 60:40 (90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan states) |
| Minimum wage | State-wise, linked to CPI-AL | Notified state-wise; no state below ₹300/day |
| Monitoring | Social audits by Gram Sabhas | Social audits plus face authentication and geo-tagging |

**Budget 2026-27 (₹ crore):** VB-G RAM G 95,692; MGNREGS 30,000 for the transition and pending dues, down 66% from 88,000 in 2025-26 (RE). The Centre released a first instalment of ₹25,863 crore to states in July 2026.

## Implementation Challenges

**1. The guarantee was rarely met, even at 100 days.** Over the past decade, households averaged about 48 days of work a year, and under 10% completed 100 days. The Economic Survey 2023-24 linked delivery to each state's institutional capacity, not just demand.

**2. Cost shift to states.** States previously funded roughly 10% of spending. Under the 60:40 split, poorer states must find much larger sums, and any work beyond the Centre's normative allocation is fully on them. Telangana has implemented the scheme but announced a Supreme Court challenge to protect state interests.

**3. Wages below notified rates.** As of December 2025, workers in 20 of 31 states and UTs received less than the notified wage. In Rajasthan, workers were paid ₹221 on average against a notified ₹281.

**4. Unemployment allowance is almost never paid.** Between 2019-20 and 2024-25, only about 8% of allowance due was paid. In 2025-26 (as of February 2026), the figure was 2%. VB-G RAM G keeps the same provision, and states still pay it.

**5. Weak social audits.** By December 2025, only 63% of 2.7 lakh Gram Panchayats had held at least one social audit that year. One Panchayat Secretary manages 17 GPs on average.

**6. Transition dues.** Worker groups estimated about ₹15,000 crore in pending MGNREGA liabilities. That could absorb half of the ₹30,000 crore transition allocation.

![Change in MGNREGA persondays by state, FY 2023-24 to FY 2024-25](../mgnrega-analysis/output/persondays_change.png)

**Findings from my analysis of FY 2024-25 data (19 states):**

- Work fell in 13 of 19 states. Nationally, persondays dropped 7.1%, from 288.8 to 268.4 crore.
- The steepest falls were in Odisha (−34.8%), Tamil Nadu (−25.1%) and Rajasthan (−15.9%). Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu also had the largest wage shortfalls, around 20% each.
- Maharashtra's work rose 39.7%, yet 63% of its registered workers are ineligible for Aadhaar-based payment. Gujarat (58%) and Jharkhand (38%) follow. Rising work does not mean everyone who needs it can get it.
- West Bengal recorded zero persondays because central funds were stopped in March 2022. It is the clearest case of how funding decisions, not demand, drive outcomes.

## Recommendations

1. **Publish normative allocations and their formula.** States should know their central ceiling before the year begins, so demand isn't turned away mid-year.
2. **A transition fund for low-capacity states.** Phase in the 40% share over 2–3 years for states whose own revenue is below the national median.
3. **Make delay compensation automatic.** Calculate it in the payment system and pay it without a claim, since manual claims have yielded 2–8% of dues.
4. **Fund Panchayat staffing for audits.** Tie a small share of administrative funds to filling Panchayat Secretary posts, with the target that every GP holds at least one social audit a year.
5. **Clear MGNREGA dues on a published schedule.** Report pending liabilities by state each quarter until they are cleared.
6. **Revise the wage base year.** The Standing Committee (2024, 2025) recommended updating the 2009-10 CPI-AL base so wages keep pace with inflation.

## Draft Parliamentary Questions

To be asked of the Minister of Rural Development:

**Q1 (Starred).** Will the Minister be pleased to state: (a) the state-wise normative allocation fixed under VB-G RAM G for 2026-27, and the criteria used; (b) the number of states that have released their full 40% matching share since 1 July 2026; and (c) the steps taken to ensure work demand is not denied once a state's normative allocation is exhausted?

**Q2 (Unstarred).** Will the Minister be pleased to state: (a) the state-wise amount of unemployment allowance and delay compensation due and paid under MGNREGA and VB-G RAM G from 2023-24 to date; and (b) whether the Government proposes to automate such payments?

**Q3 (Unstarred).** Will the Minister be pleased to state: (a) the state-wise pending liabilities under MGNREGA as of 1 July 2026; and (b) the timeline for clearing them?

## Zero Hour Note (talking points)

**Issue:** Unpaid unemployment allowance under the rural employment guarantee.

**Point to make:** The law promises an allowance when work isn't given within 15 days. Yet only 2% of what was due in 2025-26 was paid. The guarantee has now been extended to 125 days, but a promise is only as strong as its enforcement.

**Ask:** That the Ministry publish state-wise allowance dues every quarter, and pay them automatically through the payment system.
